# HEAD SHOT: sniper headshots in bullet time, with a bullet that never existed

UT99's sniper rifle is **hitscan**: no projectile exists, in the game or in the demo, and the "bullet" arrives the moment
the trigger is pulled. A HEAD SHOT film gives it one. Each shot plays the real kill from the demo: a first-person lead-in
at normal speed that ends on the trigger, then the game drops to **0.02x** while a 3D bullet flies from the muzzle to the
victim's head (air ripples trailing it, a camera riding it), lands on the exact demo frame of the kill, and the head
**bursts** (Ultra Gore mod effects) and stays gone. Every impact lands on a downbeat of the music, with the original
1999 "Headshot!" announcer.

One film was made this way (2026-11-11, 13 headshots, 90.8 s, upscaled to 3840x2160); after one review round the
author rated it "Looks great". Its shot list, thumbnail config and YouTube text are in `docs/examples/headshot/`.
Read this file before making another one; it holds the lessons of about 60 renders.

```
find headshots     python tools\find_headshots.py output\hs-candidates.json            -> pick ~15, write picks.json  (2)
probe the kills    pwsh tools\Probe-Picks.ps1 -Picks picks.json -OutDir <work>\probes                                (3)
                   python tools\hs_facts.py picks.json <work>\probes <work>\facts.json  exact kill frame, head, muzzle
write the film     sequence.json: music, bars per shot, camera template per shot                                    (6)
plan               python tools\hs_plan.py <work>\facts.json sequence.json output\plans\headshot.json
render             pwsh tools\Render-Headshots.ps1 -Plan ... -Work <work>       ~30 s per clip, game owns the screen (7)
check              python tools\sheet.py <work>\s01B.mp4 sheet.jpg 1.0 2.0 ...   contact sheets of every impact
assemble           python tools\hs_assemble.py PLAN <work> out.mp4 [--draft]    720p draft ~75 s, 2160p ~15 min  (8)
thumbnails         python tools\hs_thumbs.py <work> output\<name>                <work>\thumbs.json                (9)
```

## 1. What you need beyond the base toolkit

- **The bullet** is part of `DemoDirector.u` (`DDBullet`, `DDRing`, `DDBulletShine`; mesh + textures generated by
  `tools\make_bullet_mesh.py`, in `ut\DemoDirector\Models|Textures`). Nothing to install.
- **Gore (optional, but it is the point):** the **Ultra Gore SSE** mod by Feralidragon (version D, `NWUltraGoreSSEd.zip`,
  ~5 MB, from its ModDB page under the Nali Weapons 3 mod; ask the user before downloading, see `docs/PORTABLE.md`).
  Copy the three files of its `System` folder into `UT99\System`, then rerun `setup\Setup.ps1`: it installs
  `DDUltraGore.u` and adds it to `DDMake.ini`. Without the mod the impact gets stock UT blood.
- **The original announcer** (male, 1999) for the "Headshot!" on every impact. Many installs carry a **modded**
  `Sounds\Announcer.uax` (section 8.3). The film used the one from the author's own GOTY CD image.

## 2. Finding headshots

`tools\find_headshots.py OUT.json [--top N] [--all-players]` reads the rich scans (`output\scans\*.rich.log`). A headshot
is the kill-feed line **`X put a bullet through Y's head.`** (`Head Shot!!` is only the HUD text). For each one it takes
both players' positions and the victim's death record and scores it: distance, victim speed and air time, shooter air
time and speed; minus spawn kills (victim died < 3.7 s before), dying yourself within 3 s, and kills right after your
own respawn. Bots are skipped.

- In a large archive most headshots come from sniper-arena servers with map-embedded rifles (`ST_SniperRifle*`,
  `ST_h4x_Rifle*`), many on DM maps; only a small share are your own.
- **Scan positions are only logged ~4x per second.** Distances of fast players can be far off (a "529 uu" air-to-air
  shot was really 155 uu). Always probe (section 3) before planning.
- Good material: long shots (the film's finale was 200 m), victims flying (jump pads, dodges, falls), shooters in the
  air, both in the air.
- **Don't include kills of the author** unless they ask after seeing it. The first cut had a 307 m headshot on the
  author as a twist (they had agreed up front); after watching they removed it.

## 3. Probing: the exact frame and positions of each kill

`Probe-Kill.ps1 -Demo X.dem -T <t> -Killer A -Victim B -Out probe.json` plays the moment at 0.25x with
`ddprobe k=<id> v=<id>` on: both pawns are logged every tick (`PROBE`: location, velocity, view rotation, animation,
weapon, fov, death count) plus every new carcass near the victim (`CARC`: class, location, animation, **mesh**).
`Probe-Picks.ps1` runs it for a whole picks list (~35 s per kill). `hs_facts.py` reduces the probes:

- **Kill frame (`tk`) = the first demo frame that contains the victim's `*Carcass`.** The pawn is hidden one frame later,
  the death counter up to 0.1 s later; the kill-feed message arrives with the carcass.
- Several ticks share one 5 ms demo frame and the pawn is extrapolated between them: **take the first tick** of the kill
  frame for the victim position.
- Head = victim location + 30 uu (+14 when really crouching). `DuckWlkL` is also the falling animation: only a slow
  pawn without vertical speed is crouching.
- The facts also carry the shooter's track (to place the muzzle at the launch time), weapon, fov (scoped or not),
  speeds, distance in meters (1 uu = 0.75 inch = 0.01905 m) and the **victim's carcass mesh** (section 5.3).

## 4. The bullet: DDBullet

```
ddbullet from=x,y,z to=x,y,z tk=<demo s> dur=<wall s> [slow0= slow1= ws= scale= spin= rings= rs= rg= wake=]
         [gore=N skull=N fog= splat= mom= blood=auto explode=0|1 track=0|1 hz= vid=<id> kid=<id> gorefx=<class>]
ddbullet off                                        Render-Clip.ps1 -Bullet "<args>"
```

- Arrives at **demo time `tk`**; `dur` is the wall time of the flight. Wall time to `tk` comes from the speed ramp
  (`DDWatcher.WallBetween`). Demo time moves in steps at 0.02x, so between steps the bullet runs on real time, held
  within [TD, TD + 0.3] (same trick as DDCam). If the demo reaches `tk` first, the bullet snaps to the head: never late.
- `slow0` / `slow1` = seconds of slow flight leaving the barrel / arriving at the head (speed `ws` of cruise).
- `rings` = air ripples every N uu (`DDRing`, a translucent quad that grows and fades in real time), `rs` size,
  `rg` brightness. A camera riding *behind* the bullet never sees them: they trail behind it.
- **`track=1` (default): the end point follows the victim pawn** to where its head will be at `tk`. The planned head
  comes from a probe run; in the render run the pawn can be a few uu elsewhere. The hit log prints `trackerr`.
- After the hit it flies on to the wall behind the head. Logs `DD|BULLET|launch`, `hit`, and `DD|GORE|...`.
- The mesh is 5 uu long at `scale=1` (the film used 1.5). `Brightness=1.0` in the game washes unlit textures out: the
  generator authors them dark.

### 4.1 Meshes without a modelling tool

`tools\make_bullet_mesh.py ut\DemoDirector` writes Unreal 1 vertex meshes (`_a.3d` / `_d.3d`: 16-byte triangles,
vertices packed `x*8 | y*8 << 11 | z*4 << 22` in 11/11/10 bits, coordinates within +-127, `MESHMAP SCALE` Z = 2 x X)
and 8-bit BMP textures: a bullet with rifling grooves and cannelure, an environment-mapped shine shell, the ripple quad
and a wake puff. They are imported by the `#exec` lines in `DDBullet.uc` / `DDRing.uc`; rebuild with
`tools\Build-DD.ps1` (copies the repo sources into the UT folder, runs `ucc make`, copies the `.u` files back).

## 5. Exploding heads

### 5.1 Ultra Gore in a demo

Ultra Gore is a **server mutator**: a demo only contains the gore the original server sent, so enabling the mutator
does nothing for playback. Its effect classes can be spawned **locally** though (a locally spawned actor has
Role = Authority): `DDUltraGore.DDUltraGoreFX` does what the mutator's `setupVictimGoreFX` does for a `'Decapitated'`
kill: spawn `NWBloodyMess_UGSSE` (initialises itself from `NWUltraGoreSSE.ini`, one harmless `Accessed None 'Game'`
warning), then `NWHeadShotExplFXA/B/C_UGSSE` + `InitBloodFX('Decapitated', victim, board, mutator, "0,1,2", ...)`:
splat sprite, flying meat, cords and 10 skull fragments, blood fog, sound. The mod ships its script source
(`ucc batchexport NWUltraGoreSSEd.u class uc <dir>`).

- **DemoDirector must not depend on the mod** (the package would not load without it). `DDBullet` loads the gore
  provider **by name** (`gorefx=`, default `DDUltraGore.DDUltraGoreFX`, a subclass of `DDGoreBase` in its own package)
  and falls back to stock UT blood.
- The victim reference must not be None (fxScale 0 = nothing spawns; a zero `Victim.Scale` can even hang the mod's
  loop): use the killer or the spectator when the victim pawn is gone.
- The **blood fog** is a big flat sprite: from 60 uu it fills the frame and hides the head. `fog=0.35 splat=0.55`
  shrink the sprites (not the pieces); with `gore=2` only one fog is spawned. `skull=1` adds one more bone burst.
- The mod gives Xan (Boss) models and cyborg skins **silver** blood (grey smoke). Forced red unless `blood=auto`.
- The gore moves in demo time, so at 0.02x the pieces hang in the air and the speed ramp lets them fly.

### 5.2 Heads must burst and stay gone

The author's main correction after the first cut: *"make it happen that the heads actually explode / are hit"* and
*"ensure heads stay bursted after bullet hit"*. A red cloud over the head is not enough.

UT itself only removes a head when the damage type is `'Decapitated'`: the dying pawn plays a **headless death pose**
and throws an **intact head** chunk. In the author's demos only 2 of 13 sniper kills did that; the rest used ordinary
death poses (Dead1/2/3/5) with the head on. With `explode=1` (default) DDBullet therefore:

- **hides the intact flying head** (`UTHeads`, `TSkaarjHead`, any non-body carcass with "Head" in its class name) near
  the impact, so the head *bursts* instead of popping off whole;
- **keeps the victim's corpse in its model's headless pose** for the rest of the shot (`KeepHeadless`, re-applied every
  tick): the head vertices of these animations are collapsed into the neck from frame 0 on (checked with
  `tools\blender\ut_mesh.py read_mesh`):

| model (mesh) | headless pose | from (UT code) |
|---|---|---|
| male: Soldier, Commando; Xan: Boss | `Dead4` | `TournamentMale.PlayDecap` |
| female: FCommando, SGirl | `Dead6` | `TournamentFemale.PlayDecap` (has `Dead6`) |
| Skaarj trooper: `EpicCustomModels.TSkM` (carcass still `TMale1Carcass`) | `Dead5` | `MultiMesh.TSkaarj.PlaySkaarjDecap` |

- **Not covered:** Cow, Nali and other custom models. Check the victim's carcass mesh in the probe (`CARC ... mesh=`) and
  skip those kills, or add their decap pose (look for `PlayDecap` / `Decap` in their player class).
- Xan's tall shoulder armour can still read as a head in some frames.

### 5.3 Camera at the impact

The intact head must be **in frame before the bullet arrives**, then burst, then stay gone. `DDCam mode=bullet` keys
the rig over the flight; `ue=0.8` makes it reach its end framing at 80 % of the flight. End framing that works: side-on,
`r2=92`, `lz2=-14` (look 14 uu below the bullet line: head and chest; looking at the bullet line left the body
below the picture), `pull=130` (backs off after the hit).

## 6. Camera: DDCam `mode=bullet`

```
ddcam mode=bullet dist= r= a0= lead= h= fov=  [um= dist1= r1= a1= lead1= h1= fov1=]  [ue= dist2= r2= a2= lead2= h2= fov2=]
      [w= lz2= pull= lag=]                    any other mode: lookb=1 looks at the bullet
```

A rig fixed to the bullet: `dist` behind it (negative = ahead, looking back), `r` off the flight axis at angle `a`
(0 = right, 90 = above, 180 = left), `lead` look-ahead. Values ease from the start keys to the middle keys (at flight
fraction `um`) to the end keys (at `ue`). After the hit it stays at the head. Templates in `hs_plan.py`:

- **classic**: macro at the muzzle, then ahead of the bullet looking back along the ripple trail, then side-on at the head.
- **rider**: behind the bullet with the target ahead, then side-on at the head.
- **duel**: side-on with both players in frame, for point-blank shots (3 m). For 20 m it was too wide: the burst was tiny.
- `side=180` puts the rig on the shooter's left. **"OBS capture still black after restart"** on a bullet clip = the
  muzzle camera starts inside a wall next to the shooter: switch sides.

**Reveal lead-in** (`"lead": "reveal"`): instead of the first-person lead-in the camera starts 260 uu in front of the
target and dollies back along the line of fire (free of walls by definition) to over the shooter's shoulder, with a
distance counter. Used for the 200 m finale, whose first-person view was a wall with a window slit.

## 7. Planning and rendering

`sequence.json` (example in `docs/examples/headshot/`): music (`file` in `music\`, `bar` length, `drop` = music time
of the first impact), intro/outro bars, and per shot `bars` = [lead-in, flight, aftermath], `cam`, `side`, `gore`,
`lead`. `hs_plan.py` turns it plus the facts into jobs: per shot an **A clip** (first person at 1x, ending on the
trigger) and a **B clip** (bullet time), shot starts and impacts on the music grid, the speed ramp and the
`ddbullet` / `ddcam` arguments.

- **Measure the bar length from the audio.** `beats.py` drifted 0.2 s over the track; onset autocorrelation over 8/16
  bars gave Phantom's bar as 2.162 s (111 BPM).
- B ramp: `t0-0.16:1, t0-0.06:0.02, tk:0.02, tk+0.03:0.12, tk+0.25:0.3, tk+0.8:1`. **A clip that starts at 0.02x
  cannot be reached by a seek** (it lands ~0.05 s late, and the retry is a slow backward seek), so the ramp starts at
  1x and drops just before the clip start.
- `Render-Headshots.ps1 -Plan -Work [-Only s01B,s05B] [-Force]`: resumable, one game launch per clip, one retry.
  From `pwsh -File`, `-Only a,b,c` arrives as one string; the script splits it.
- **Check every impact on a contact sheet** (`tools\sheet.py CLIP OUT.jpg t1 t2 ... [--cols] [--w] [--crop x,y,w,h]`):
  head in frame before the hit, burst, headless after. ffmpeg `drawtext` may crash without fontconfig; the sheet
  tool labels frames with Pillow.
- Stopping a batch mid-clip can leave a clip without its `.json` and a raw `.mkv` in `recordings\`.
- **True 4K capture** works (the game renders 3840x2160 with `WindowedViewportX/Y`, OBS captures it) but on a notebook
  iGPU the encoder dropped 194 frames in 12 s. The film was captured at 1080p and **upscaled** at assembly.

## 8. Assembly

`hs_assemble.py PLAN WORK OUT.mp4 [--draft] [--only-audio]`.

### 8.1 Picture
1080p clips -> Lanczos + `cas=0.45` sharpening to 3840x2160 (`--draft`: 1280x720). Lead-ins natural; flights graded
cold green with letterbox; colour returns at the impact. The UI layer is drawn natively at output size and piped per
frame: shooter / target names, a distance countdown from the same distance table as DDBullet, range bar, "TIME x 0.02"
with the real .308 flight time, and on the impact a HEAD SHOT title with killer > victim, distance and target speed.
Intro: a console typing "projectile: none (hitscan)" ... "slomo 0.02". Outro: "N HEADSHOTS. 0 BULLETS EXISTED." and
credits (the mod by name).

### 8.2 Sound
The music (a UT module from `music\`, decoded with libopenmpt) is low-passed under each flight and slams back on the
impact = downbeat; synthesized freeze, whoosh, heartbeat and impact; the announcer 0.16 s after every impact; game
audio of the clips. `--only-audio` rebuilds `mix.wav` in ~20 s; mux it with `ffmpeg -c:v copy` instead of re-rendering.
**Measure the encoded file**: a mix at -0.4 dBTP as WAV came out at +3.6 dBTP after AAC, always on the impacts (a noise
crack through a soft limiter). A limiter afterwards did not help; lowering the crack and the impact gain did.

### 8.3 The announcer must be the original
The author: *"use the original male 'Headshot!' announcer, NOT the female one"*. The `Announcer.uax` and
`AnnouncerX.uax` on the recording install were **modded**: every kill-streak voice was a 44/48 kHz replacement. The
original 1999 pack has all its voices at **22 kHz 8-bit** (`monsterkill` at 11 kHz). Check with
`ucc batchexport ..\Sounds\Announcer.uax sound wav <dir>` and the sample rates. The original came from the author's
UT GOTY CD image (official free copy, archive.org item `ut-goty`, `UT_GOTY_CD1.iso`, MD5 e5127537f44086f5ed36a9d29f992c00,
approved by Epic and used by the OldUnreal installer; **ask the user before reading anything from the internet**):
`tools\iso_fetch.py <iso or URL> SOUNDS/ANNOUN~1.UAX Announcer.uax` copies just that 1.4 MB file (HTTP range requests
for a URL; ISO9660 short names). Export it into `<assetsDir>\sounds\announcer_orig\`. Never replace the game's own file.

## 9. Thumbnails and the YouTube text

`hs_thumbs.py WORK PREFIX` with `WORK\thumbs.json`: (1) "YOU CAN HEAR THIS IMAGE" + a big red "HEAD SHOT!!" over an
impact (the announcer is the nostalgia hook), (2) "1999 / HITSCAN. 0.000 s" vs "<year> / BULLET TIME" split with a
pixelated, CRT left half, (3) bullet macro with VHS "REC" furniture and the longest distance. No game logos.
The author asked for UHD, three nostalgic thumbnails, victim names on screen and the announcer: nostalgia sells.
`docs/examples/headshot/youtube-example.txt` is the published title, description, chapters and tags.

## 10. Example: the 2026-11-11 film

`docs/examples/headshot/sequence.json` is the final shot list (13 shots, Phantom, 90.8 s). Shot notes in it record
the swaps: a kill of the author was removed after review, an air-to-air kill was replaced because its victim's model
